Skills & Evidence

Five skills I developed and evidenced

For each skill I give a short description, how I built it during the module, and the evidence that supports it.

Skill 01

Communication

I learned to present ideas clearly to different audiences, from informal peer discussions to a structured pitch. I practised adjusting my language, pace and visuals so the message landed with investors and tutors alike.

EvidencePitch slides, recorded presentation clip, and tutor feedback notes on clarity and delivery.
Skill 02

Teamwork & Collaboration

Working in a group to shape the venture taught me to share tasks fairly, listen to different viewpoints and reach agreement under time pressure. I took responsibility for my part while supporting others.

EvidenceShared task board, meeting photographs, and a group agreement document signed by all members.
Skill 03

Problem Solving

When our first idea did not stand up to market research, I helped the team analyse what went wrong and reshape the concept. I used structured thinking to weigh options rather than guessing.

EvidenceProblem/solution canvas, a revised business model sketch, and research notes showing the pivot.
Skill 04

Leadership & Initiative

I stepped up to coordinate deadlines and keep the group focused. Leading by example, I set small milestones and checked progress so the venture stayed on track to the pitch date.

EvidenceProject plan with milestones, a progress tracker, and peer comments on how I kept the team organised.
Skill 05

Creativity & Innovation

Generating a fresh idea and refining it into a workable venture built my creative confidence. I used idea-generation methods to move past the obvious and test what made our offer different.

EvidenceIdea-generation posters, mood board, and the final concept summary for the new venture.
Skill 06

Digital & Presentation

Building this webpage and the pitch deck improved my digital confidence. I learned to combine text, images and structure so that information is easy to follow and professional in appearance.

EvidenceThis published webpage, the slide deck, and screenshots of the drafting process.
The New Venture

Where the skills were applied

The skills above were not developed in isolation. They were built while my team created and presented a new venture, from first idea to final pitch.

💡

The idea

A clear problem, a target customer, and a simple offer that answers a real need in the market.

📊

Market research

We tested demand, studied competitors and refined the concept based on what the evidence told us.

🎤

The pitch

We presented the venture with a confident, well-structured pitch supported by clear visuals.

Reflection

Developing a new venture pushed me to use employability skills in a real, pressured setting rather than in theory. I grew most in communication and leadership, because the pitch and the group deadlines gave me genuine reasons to improve.

Looking ahead, I will keep strengthening my commercial awareness and digital skills, since both will matter in any enterprise or graduate role I move into next.

  • Strongest growth: Communication and leadership under real deadlines.
  • Key lesson: Evidence is stronger when collected as you go, not at the end.
  • Next focus: Commercial awareness and financial planning.
  • Transfer: These skills apply directly to graduate and start-up roles.
